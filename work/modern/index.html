How we do it

Modern doors, done right the first time.

Modern doors drop the raised panels for smooth, flat sections. That clean face lets color and glass do the work: black or charcoal against light siding, a stack of windows down one side, or long horizontal lites across the top.

Wood-look finishes are a big part of the modern look too. A plank-pattern steel door in walnut or light oak warms up a dark exterior and pairs well with stone and board-and-batten, without the staining and sealing real wood needs.

Modern doors show every gap and uneven line, so the install matters. We square the opening, set the track and trim tight, and adjust the door so the seams line up when it's closed.

Questions

Common questions

Do glass garage doors let heat out?

Glass lets more heat through than an insulated steel section, so a full-glass door is best on garages you don't heat hard. Insulated glass and doors with fewer lites are a good middle ground. We'll walk you through the trade-off.

Will a dark door fade or get too hot?

Dark factory finishes are made for sun exposure, but dark doors do absorb more heat. On a south- or west-facing door, we'll point you to finishes and models rated for it.

Can a modern door go on an older house?

Often, yes. A flush or wood-look door can modernize an older front without a full remodel. The Door Builder on our homepage is a quick way to try it against your siding color.

Are estimates really free?

Yes. We'll measure, show you options and give you a written price at no charge.
